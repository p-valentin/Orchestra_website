# Beta licence email — SENT 2026-07-29 to 17 recipients

Sent 2026-07-29 16:16 UTC to the 17 unclaimed lifetime licences, 17/17
accepted by Resend, one message each (no BCC), recorded in `public.sent_emails`
with source `beta-launch`. The text below is exactly what went out.

The one thing this email has to get right is the **exact address**. A licence
attaches by matching the email on the account to the email on the licence. If
someone signs up with a different address — a work one, a `+tag`, a different
Gmail — the licence stays unclaimed and they land on a 14-day trial instead,
with no error to tell them why. That is why the address is repeated rather than
mentioned once.

## Subject

```
Your Orchestra lifetime licence is ready
```

Alternatives, if that reads too much like a receipt:
- `Orchestra 1.2 is out — your licence is waiting`
- `Claiming your Orchestra licence`

## Body (plain text — the compose box adds the wrapper)

```
Hi,

Orchestra is live, and the lifetime licence you were promised is waiting for you.

To claim it, create an account using this exact email address — the one this
message was sent to:

  https://www.orchestra-automation.com/signup

The licence attaches itself the moment the account exists. There is no key to
copy and nothing to activate. Sign in inside the app and it is simply yours,
on up to 3 machines, with no expiry and no subscription.

One thing worth saying plainly: it has to be this address. If you sign up with
a different one, the licence will not find you and you will land on a trial
instead. If you would rather it were attached to a different address, reply to
this email and I will move it.

If you do not already have the latest build:

  https://www.orchestra-automation.com/downloads

Thank you for the early testing. A lot of what shipped is a direct result of
it — including several things that only broke on someone else's machine.

Valentin
Orchestra
```

## Notes on choices

**"the one this message was sent to"** — most people will not know which
address they gave months ago. Naming it relative to the message they are
reading removes the guesswork without printing their address in the body.

**Offering to move it** — someone will want it on a different address, and the
alternative to offering is them signing up wrong and quietly getting a trial.
Cheaper to handle 2 replies than 2 confused refund requests.

**No mention of the price, the refund window, or Polar.** They are not buying
anything; a payment-shaped email would be confusing and would make some of them
check their card statement.

**No licence key anywhere** — the account is the licence. Including a key-like
string would create a second source of truth and invite support questions about
where to paste it.
